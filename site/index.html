On authorship, the creative process, and becoming.

My work is concerned with authorship: how we construct meaning, how we come to understand ourselves, and how we participate in shaping the lives and work that are our own.

I work with artists exploring what moves through their creative process, and with women navigating the complex territory between one understanding of themselves and another.

01

The stories we live by.

I am interested in the conclusions we draw from our experiences. The roles we repeatedly inhabit. The beliefs that have become so familiar we rarely think to question them.

Much of what we understand to be our identity is made of interpretations — of ourselves, of other people, of what has happened to us, and of what we believe is possible.

Over time, these interpretations acquire authority. They begin to determine what we expect, what we choose, what we create, and what we allow ourselves to become.

A central part of my work is making these structures visible.

Through conversation, psychological inquiry, symbolic exploration, and attention to the body, I accompany people in examining the patterns and assumptions that organize their experience.

We investigate what is repeating, what has become difficult to reconcile, and what might be asking for a different understanding.

02

The work we make.

My work with artists brings this inquiry directly into the creative process.

I am interested in the relationship between the maker and what they make.

Why does a particular image keep returning? What is someone trying to articulate before they have the language for it? What draws them toward a certain subject? What becomes difficult when an idea begins to acquire form?

The creative process offers an extraordinary opportunity for self-knowledge. It confronts us with our own choices, instincts, contradictions, limitations, and desires.

And yet, the work matters in itself.

An image, a text, a film, or a body of work deserves to exist on its own terms. It asks for discernment, formal precision, and the willingness to remain with something long enough for it to become what it needs to be.

We discover ourselves through what we make. We also become different through the making.

She Who Becomes

For women at thresholds.

An inquiry into identity, meaning, and the experience of becoming.

There are periods when our existing understanding of ourselves becomes inadequate, when the old ways of living no longer feel possible, and something new has yet to find its form.

These moments invite a reconsideration of the stories we live by, the identities we have inherited, and the authority we exercise in our own lives.

Two ways to participate: a 12-week journey and an ongoing membership with weekly gatherings.

04

On the nature of my work

Across both my personal and creative practice, I return to the same questions.

  • What have we come to believe about ourselves?
  • Which meanings and expectations have we inherited, and which have we consciously chosen?
  • What is trying to find expression?
  • And what becomes possible when we begin to recognize our own authority in the lives we lead and the things we create?

For me, authorship is both a psychological capacity and a creative practice.

It is the ongoing work of developing a more conscious relationship with our experience, our imagination, and the forms through which we give our lives meaning.
