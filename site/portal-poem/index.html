Portal Poem

Artistic authorship, the imagination, and the creative process.

ii

An image returns.

What are you trying to bring into existence?

There is the work we imagine, and there is the work that begins to exist when we give it our attention.

Between the two, something happens.

An idea acquires form. An image returns. A sentence takes us somewhere we hadn't intended to go. We begin to recognize a language, a preoccupation, a particular way of seeing that could not have been fully understood beforehand.

Portal Poem is devoted to this relationship between the maker and what they make.

It is a place to investigate the creative process, develop your own artistic language, and give substance to what is asking to be expressed.

iii

Why does an image persist?

The work as a form of knowledge

  • What draws you repeatedly toward a particular subject?
  • Why does an image persist?
  • What do your aesthetic instincts reveal about how you perceive and organize the world?
  • What becomes difficult when an idea begins to take form?

The creative process brings us into contact with aspects of ourselves that reflection alone may never reveal.

We encounter our taste, our contradictions, our relationship to judgment, our imagination, and the limits of what we currently understand.

Sometimes we find what we were looking for. Sometimes the work takes us somewhere entirely unexpected.

We discover ourselves through what we make, but we also become different through the making.

iv

The integrity of the work

I don't believe art exists merely to reveal something about its maker.

The work has its own integrity.

A piece of writing, an image, a film, or a body of work deserves serious attention to its form, language, structure, and particular demands.

My role is to accompany both the maker and the work.

We investigate what is emerging, but we also make decisions. We develop the material, sharpen its expression, examine its structure, and look for the form in which it can most fully exist.

I am interested in the relationship between self-knowledge and artistic discernment, between what moves through us and what we consciously choose to make of it.

The 12-week journey

Private creative mentorship

Bring me the thing you cannot finish.

A sustained collaboration around a creative project, a developing body of work, or an idea that has yet to find its form.

Over twelve weeks, we work closely with your material and the questions it raises.

We explore what you're trying to make, the language and aesthetic choices emerging from it, and the places where the work asks for greater clarity, courage, precision, or commitment.

The mentorship combines six private 90-minute sessions with independent creative work, assignments, and written feedback between our meetings.

The intention is twofold: to bring something substantial into existence, and to leave you with a more developed understanding of your creative process and artistic judgment.

vi

creation is rarely so obedient.

A note on authorship

We often imagine that an artist begins with a clear intention and then finds the means to express it.

But creation is rarely so obedient.

The work changes as we make it. It challenges our intentions, exposes contradictions, and occasionally reveals something we had no idea we were trying to say.

Authorship is not absolute control over the work.

It is the development of a conscious, discerning relationship with the creative process: knowing when to follow, when to question, when to revise, and when to give something its final form.

The work matters in itself.

And something happens to us in the attempt to make it.
