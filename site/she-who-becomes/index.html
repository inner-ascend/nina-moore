She Who Becomes

Psychological authorship, identity, and the experience of becoming.

When the life you know no longer tells you who you are.

There are moments when our existing understanding of ourselves becomes inadequate.

The roles we have inhabited, the convictions we have lived by, the futures we imagined begin to lose their certainty.

Sometimes something dramatic has happened. Sometimes nothing outwardly remarkable has changed, but we find ourselves increasingly unfamiliar within the life we have built.

She Who Becomes is a space devoted to these moments.

To the questions that arise when one understanding of ourselves is coming to an end and another has yet to find its form.

01

The stories we live by

Much of what we understand to be our identity is made of interpretations.

Of what happened to us. Of what others have said about us. Of the roles we learned to inhabit. Of what we believe we are capable of, deserving of, or destined to become.

Over time, these interpretations acquire authority.

We begin to organize our choices, relationships, and expectations around them, often without realizing that they are interpretations at all.

My work is concerned with making these structures visible.

  • What have you come to believe about yourself?
  • What keeps repeating?
  • Which identities have you inherited, and which have you chosen?
  • What happens when the story you have lived by no longer explains your experience?
02

The threshold

There is a particular kind of disorientation that accompanies profound change.

You may still understand the language of your former life while no longer feeling entirely at home within it.

You may recognize that something is changing without yet knowing what to do with that knowledge.

You may be deeply attached to the person you have been, even as you realize that certain ways of living are no longer possible.

I am interested in this territory.

Not because every uncertainty contains a hidden revelation, but because these periods invite us to examine the assumptions through which we have understood ourselves.

They ask us to reconsider identity, meaning, desire, and personal authority.

And to remain in relationship with what is emerging before we can fully explain it.

03

How we inquire

We begin with lived experience.

Through conversation, psychological inquiry, symbolic exploration, reflection, and attention to the body, we follow what is present.

We investigate the patterns that repeat, the emotions that demand attention, the contradictions that resist easy resolution.

We look at how certain experiences became conclusions about who we are, and how those conclusions continue to shape our lives.

The purpose is not to replace one definitive explanation with another.

It is to become more discerning in our relationship with the meanings we have constructed, and more conscious of how we participate in what comes next.

A sustained inquiry into identity and becoming

05

On becoming

We often describe change as though it were a linear progression from an old self toward a better one.

But becoming is rarely so simple.

We carry earlier versions of ourselves into the present. We remain attached to ways of living that no longer fit. We recognize a truth before we are ready to live according to it.

There can be grief, contradiction, tenderness, resistance, and genuine uncertainty.

The work is not to produce a more acceptable version of ourselves.

It is to develop a more conscious relationship with our experience, our choices, and the meanings through which we understand our lives.
